# Method — Block Periodization (Issurin / Rønnestad)

Also called **Concentrated Loading**, or in cycling specifically **"Rønnestad HIIT blocks"** or **"front-loaded VO2max blocks"**.

## Origin

**Vladimir Issurin** (Israel / USSR) formalized block-periodization theory in the 1980s–2000s and published it in English in *Block Periodization: Breakthrough in Sport Training* (2008). **Bent Rønnestad** (Inland Norway University) translated the concept to endurance cycling with seminal RCTs starting **2012**, with continued work through 2025.

## Core principle

**Concentrate one targeted training stimulus** (e.g. VO2max HIIT) into a short, dense **mesocycle-block**, then exploit the "residual training effect" while the next block focuses on a different ability. Instead of training many qualities in parallel, you **front-load one quality at a time**.

## Weekly intensity distribution

Highly bimodal **during the block week** itself:
- **5 HIT sessions in 7 days** → ~30–40% of weekly time at high intensity
- Rest of the week: **strictly easy**

Across a full 4-week cycle, the overall distribution still resembles polarized — just front-loaded into the first week.

## Hallmark sessions

- **The "5 HIT in 7 days" block-week**: `6 × 5 min @ ~95% HRmax` with 2.5 min recovery, run 5 times in a week.
- **Short-interval HIT**: `3 × (13 × 30s on / 15s off)` at maximal sustainable power (Rønnestad's short-interval study, 2020).
- **Maintenance week sessions**: 1 HIT day + easy volume, repeated for 3 weeks before the next block.

## Periodization

Concentrated **1–4 week blocks**, sequenced as **Accumulation → Transformation → Realization** mesocycles (Issurin). Rønnestad's cycling protocol: **1 HIT-block week + 3 weeks 1×/week HIT maintenance**, repeated.

## Profile fit

Well-trained competitive cyclists / triathletes (**8–20 h/week**) with a stable aerobic base, in a **build phase 6–10 weeks out from peak**; particularly effective for plateaued athletes needing a VO2max kick.

## Anti-fit

Beginners without an aerobic base. Athletes still ramping volume. Anyone in a heavy life-stress period — block weeks have a **high acute fatigue cost** and require disciplined recovery. **Not appropriate as a year-round model.**

## How this skill applies it

When designing workouts under this method:

1. **Block-context is critical.** Ask which phase the athlete is in: *block week*, *maintenance week*, or *base*. The same request means different sessions.
2. **Block week** — design `4–6 × 5min @ 95% HRmax` (or `3 × (13×30s/15s)`) and prefix the title with `[Block]`. Stack five of these in the week; the 5 HIT days own intensity, all other days are recovery-easy.
3. **Maintenance week** — one HIT session per week, identical structure but lower frequency. The other 6 days are easy.
4. **Recovery/easy** sessions during a block week are non-negotiable Z1 — cap at upper Z2. Athlete is already at high training load.
5. Strongly recommend a **deload week** every 4–6 weeks; surface a reminder if the athlete asks for a 4th consecutive HIT-block.
6. Predominantly **cycling-centric** in research, but applies to running and ski with appropriate session design. For swimming, fall back to default methods.
