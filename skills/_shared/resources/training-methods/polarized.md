# Method — Polarized 80/20 (Seiler)

Popularly called **"80/20 training"** or the **Seiler Model**.

## Origin

**Stephen Seiler** (American exercise physiologist at the University of Agder, Norway) coined the term in the early 2000s after observing elite Scandinavian endurance athletes. Landmark paper: **Seiler & Kjerland 2006** in *Scand J Med Sci Sports*. Popularized for amateurs by **Matt Fitzgerald**'s *80/20 Running* (2014).

## Core principle

Spend **~80% of training time at low intensity** (below LT1 / VT1) and **~20% at genuinely high intensity** (above LT2 / VT2), with **minimal time in the moderate "grey zone"** between the two thresholds.

## Weekly intensity distribution

Bimodal by session count, not just by time:
- **~80%** Zone 1 (easy, below VT1)
- **~0–5%** Zone 2 (between thresholds — the grey zone, avoided)
- **~15–20%** Zone 3 (above VT2)

## Hallmark sessions

- **4 × 8 min @ ~95% HRmax / VO2max**, 2 min recovery.
- **4–6 × 4 min @ VO2max**, equal-time recovery.
- Long slow distance **60–180 min strictly below VT1** (talk-test pace).

## Periodization

Generally undulating within the week (one or two hard days, the rest easy). Macro structure is flexible — works with linear base→build→peak or reverse models.

## Profile fit

Endurance athletes training **6–20+ h/week**, especially those preparing for events lasting 30 min to several hours. Well-supported by RCTs (Stöggl & Sperlich 2014) for VO2max and time-to-exhaustion gains.

## Anti-fit

Very short-event athletes (sub-5-min efforts), pure sprinters, and time-starved athletes under ~4 h/week who genuinely cannot afford to "waste" volume at low intensity.

## How this skill applies it

When designing workouts under this method:

1. Bias each week toward **one or two genuinely hard sessions + easy everything else**. No "medium" days.
2. For interval sessions, use **Zone 5 (VO2max, ~106–120% FTP)** — not threshold. Classic 4×8 / 4×4 / 6×5 patterns.
3. Easy sessions are capped at **Zone 2 upper bound** (75% FTP / equivalent pace / CSS). If the athlete asks for "Z2 with some tempo", flag the conflict — that's the grey zone.
4. Label interval blocks `"Z5 VO2max"`; label long easy blocks `"Z1-Z2 strictly easy"` and add a description: **"keep HR/power below the upper Z2 cap; this is not a tempo ride."**
5. Avoid `STEADY` blocks in the tempo / sweet spot range — split into easier or harder.
