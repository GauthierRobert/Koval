# Method — Sweet Spot / FasCat

Also called **Sweet Spot Training (SST)**, **threshold-focused** or **high-volume tempo** training.

## Origin

**Frank Overton**, founder of **FasCat Coaching** (Boulder, CO), coined the term around 2004 in collaboration with **Andy Coggan**'s power-zone framework. Mass-popularized through **TrainerRoad**'s structured plans and the FasCat blog/podcast.

## Core principle

The **"sweet spot"** lives between high Zone 3 and low Zone 4 — **84–97% FTP** — high enough to drive meaningful FTP and aerobic-power adaptation, low enough to recover from quickly. Goal: **maximize TSS per hour** of available training time.

## Weekly intensity distribution

Threshold-heavy:
- **~60–70%** easy
- **~20–30%** sweet-spot / threshold (Z3–low Z4)
- **~5–10%** above threshold

Higher mid-intensity share than polarized. Some practitioners label it "threshold-heavy" rather than pyramidal.

## Hallmark sessions

- **2–3 × 15–20 min @ 88–94% FTP** (FasCat names: "Tallac", "Antelope", "Tunnabora").
- **Over-under intervals**: alternating 90% / 105% FTP, 2 min / 1 min.
- **4 × 10–12 min @ 90–95% FTP** with 5 min recovery.

## Periodization

Linear, phased build: FasCat's *Sweet Spot Part 1/2/3* → *Climbing* → *Race*. TrainerRoad uses *Base → Build → Specialty*, with sustained-power Base plans driven by SST.

## Profile fit

Time-crunched cyclists (**4–10 h/week**), masters athletes, indoor trainer users, and gran-fondo / road racers who need raw FTP gains and can't afford huge Zone 2 volumes.

## Anti-fit

High-volume cyclists (>15 h/week) — SST stacks too much accumulated strain at that volume. Pure sprinters/track racers. Athletes preparing for ultra-endurance events where Zone 2 fat-oxidation work matters more.

## How this skill applies it

When designing workouts under this method:

1. Default cycling target is **`STEADY` or `INTERVAL` @ 88–94% FTP**. The block label should literally say `"Sweet Spot"`.
2. Prefer **longer reps with short rest** (e.g. 3×15min @ 90% FTP, 3min rest) over short hard reps.
3. Over-under intervals are a signature — feel free to design `3 × (10min alternating 1min @ 105% / 2min @ 88%)`.
4. Warmup short and crisp (10–15 min), cooldown short (5–10 min). Time-crunched athletes don't want a 25-min warmup.
5. Predominantly **bike-specific** — if the athlete also runs/swims, fall back to default methods for those sports.
6. Aim for **TSS/hour ≥ 60** on sweet-spot days; flag the workout if it drops below that.
