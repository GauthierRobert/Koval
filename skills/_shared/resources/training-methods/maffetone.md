# Method — Maffetone (MAF / 180-Formula)

Also called the **MAF Method** or simply **"low-HR training"**.

## Origin

**Dr. Phil Maffetone**, applied kinesiologist and endurance coach, developed the method through clinical practice in the 1980s. Famously used by **Mark Allen** during his six Ironman World Championship wins (1989–1995).

## Core principle

Build the aerobic engine by training **strictly under a maximum aerobic heart rate (MAF HR)** for months at a time — improving fat oxidation, capillary density, and injury resilience **before** introducing any anaerobic work.

**MAF HR formula**: `180 − age`, then adjust:
- `−10` if recovering from illness / on medication
- `−5` if injured, regressing, asthmatic, or > 2 colds/flus per year
- `+5` only if training consistently 2+ years with no injuries/illness and racing well

## Weekly intensity distribution

During the **base phase**, essentially **100% at or below MAF HR** (Zone 1–low Zone 2). Anaerobic work is added **only** after a measurable plateau in the **MAF Test** (pace at MAF HR over a fixed distance, monthly).

## Hallmark sessions

- **MAF runs/rides of any duration**, HR capped at the MAF number — walk if needed to stay under.
- **The MAF Test**: 5 km or 5-mile time trial at MAF HR, repeated monthly. Faster pace at the same HR = aerobic improvement.
- **Long aerobic builds**: 90–180+ min under cap.

## Periodization

Reverse-flavored: extensive aerobic base first (often **3–6 months**), anaerobic work introduced sparingly and only when aerobic progress stalls.

## Profile fit

Beginners, athletes returning from injury or burnout, ultra-distance and long-course triathletes, time-rich athletes who can absorb high volume at low intensity, and HR-monitor-only users.

## Anti-fit

Short-course racers (5 k, criterium, sprint tri) needing top-end speed. Athletes with a genuinely low max HR where `180−age` feels artificially restrictive. Anyone without the patience for 3+ months of slow running.

## How this skill applies it

When designing workouts under this method:

1. **Forbidden:** Z3, Z4, Z5 sessions during the base phase. If the athlete requests one, redesign as a long aerobic session and explain why, citing MAF.
2. Every session prescribes an **HR ceiling** in the description: `"Cap HR at <MAF> bpm — walk if needed."` Use the athlete's age + adjustment from their profile to compute MAF if it's stored.
3. Use `STEADY` blocks @ low intensity (e.g. cycling 55–65% FTP / running 70–80% threshold pace / swimming Z1–Z2 CSS).
4. Schedule a **monthly MAF Test** as a structured session: warmup + 5 km (or 5-mile) time trial at MAF HR + cooldown. Persist as a recurring session.
5. After the athlete plateaus or after 3+ months base (whichever first), allow **one** structured anaerobic session per week — but flag the transition explicitly.
6. If the athlete's sport is cycling-only and they have an FTP but no HR target, fall back to **HR-equivalent power** (~55–65% FTP) and still cap by HR.
