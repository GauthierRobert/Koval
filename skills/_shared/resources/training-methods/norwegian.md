# Method — Norwegian Method (Double Threshold)

Also called the Norwegian Double-Threshold model, Sub-Threshold Training, or the Bakken Method.

## Origin

Codified by **Marius Bakken** (Norwegian 5,000 m record holder) in the early-2000s through thousands of self-administered lactate tests. Brought to global attention by the **Ingebrigtsen brothers** (Henrik, Filip, Jakob) and by triathletes **Kristian Blummenfelt** and **Gustav Iden** under coach Olav Aleksander Bu.

## Core principle

Accumulate large volumes of work **just below the second lactate threshold (LT2)**, guided by frequent finger-prick lactate measurements (typically capped at **2.5–3.5 mmol/L**). The stimulus is high but the recovery cost stays low, so the work can be repeated — including twice in the same day.

## Weekly intensity distribution

Pyramidal-leaning:
- **~85–88%** easy (below LT1)
- **~10–12%** sub-threshold (LT1–LT2)
- **~2–3%** truly hard (above LT2)

Two **double-threshold days per week** (AM + PM sessions) anchor the structure.

## Hallmark sessions

- AM track threshold reps: **5–6 × 2 km @ ~2.5 mmol/L lactate** (~88–92% threshold pace / ~88–92% FTP).
- PM threshold reps: **10–20 × 400 m** or **25 × 200 m** on short rest, same lactate cap.
- Long easy aerobic run/ride, conversational.

## Periodization

Year-round consistency with phased emphasis: aerobic base → double-threshold block → race-specific VO2/speed sharpening → competition. Altitude camps (Sierra Nevada, Font Romeu, St. Moritz) act as mesocycle anchors.

## Profile fit

Intermediate-to-elite athletes training **8+ hours/week** who can access a lactate meter, run/ride twice a day, and value precision over heroics.

## Anti-fit

Beginners, athletes under ~6 h/week, anyone without lactate testing capability, athletes who dislike pace/HR discipline. Running too hard during sub-threshold reps **breaks the model immediately**.

## How this skill applies it

When designing workouts under this method:

1. **Cap interval intensity at ~88–92%** of threshold (FTP / threshold pace / CSS). Do NOT prescribe Z5/VO2 unless the request is explicitly for a sharpening session.
2. Prefer **many short-to-medium reps with brief recovery** (e.g. 6×8min @ 90% FTP, 1min rest) over a few long maximal efforts.
3. Two interval days per week max; when persisting double-threshold days, label them `"AM Threshold"` / `"PM Threshold"`.
4. Add `description` notes asking the athlete to **stop the session if RPE climbs above conversational threshold**, since lactate-guided pacing is the safety valve.
5. Long easy sessions stay strictly Z1–Z2 — no surges, no Strava segments.
