# Method — Lydiard Method

Also called the **Lydiard System**; his own term for it was "pyramid training" (predating the modern statistical use of that label).

## Origin

**Arthur Lydiard** (New Zealand, 1917–2004). Coached **Peter Snell**, **Murray Halberg** and **Barry Magee** to Olympic medals in 1960; revolutionized distance running by proving high-mileage aerobic base benefits even 800 m runners.

## Core principle

Maximize aerobic capacity through **sustained high-mileage base running first**, then layer hill resistance, anaerobic intervals, and competition sharpening in **distinct phases** — never skipping or shortening the base.

## Weekly intensity distribution

Phase-dependent (not a single weekly recipe):

- **Marathon Conditioning (Base)** — ~95% aerobic, "best aerobic effort" steady runs, ~100 mpw classical target.
- **Hill Resistance** — ~80% aerobic + bounding / springing / downhill striding.
- **Anaerobic Development** — interval volume up to ~15–20% of weekly mileage.
- **Coordination / Sharpening** — time trials and race-pace work added.

## Hallmark sessions

- **22-mile / 35 km Sunday long run** at strong aerobic pace.
- **Hill circuit**: steep bounding + springing + downhill striding for 4 weeks.
- **Anaerobic phase intervals**: e.g. **50 × 200 m**, or repetition 400s, followed by coordination time trials.

## Periodization

Classic **linear / phased** periodization: Marathon Conditioning → Hill Resistance → Anaerobic Development → Coordination/Sharpening → Taper & Race. Typical full cycle **24+ weeks**.

## Profile fit

Committed distance runners (10 k to marathon) training 5–7 days/week with time to **build seasonally**; coaches who plan in months not weeks; high-school and collegiate programs.

## Anti-fit

Time-crunched athletes (under ~5 h/week), pure speed/sprint athletes, and athletes whose seasons don't allow a 10–14 week uninterrupted base.

## How this skill applies it

When designing workouts under this method:

1. **Phase awareness is critical.** Read the active phase from `athlete-profile.md` or ask the athlete which phase they're in. The same request ("5×1k") means different sessions in base vs. anaerobic phase.
2. **Base phase**: design steady aerobic runs at "best aerobic effort" — roughly **75–85% threshold pace**, no intervals harder than Z2.
3. **Hill phase**: use `INTERVAL` blocks with a `description` of the bound / spring / striding drill rather than power/pace targets. Add a `STEADY` jog between.
4. **Anaerobic phase**: classic repeats (5–10×1k @ threshold, 20×400m, 50×200m). Heavier interval volume than other methods.
5. **Sharpening**: short fast reps (R-pace in Daniels' language), 200m strides, race-pace time trials.
6. Long runs grow incrementally up to **22 miles / 35 km** in late base — don't cap at marathon-only volumes if the athlete is a 5k/10k runner.
7. Primarily **run-specific**; for cross-training sports, fall back to default methods.
